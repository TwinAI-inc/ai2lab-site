NEW CONCEPT

The t-Distribution

When $\sigma$ is unknown and we estimate it with $s$, extra uncertainty makes the tails heavier.

SHAPE
Symmetric, bell-shaped, but with heavier tails than Normal
CONVERGENCE
As df $\to \infty$, $t \to z$. By df=30, they're nearly identical
WHEN TO USE
$\sigma$ unknown → estimate with $s$ → use $t$ with df = $n{-}1$

INTUITION

Degrees of Freedom

DEFINITION
$$\text{df} = n - 1$$
The number of values that are free to vary after we fix the sample mean.
INTUITION
If you know $\bar{x}$ and $n{-}1$ of the values, the last value is determined. It has no freedom.
More df → more information → t-distribution closer to z.

CONFIDENCE INTERVAL

CI for One Mean (σ Unknown)

When σ is unknown, we replace it with the sample standard deviation $s$ and use the t-distribution with $n{-}1$ degrees of freedom.

FORMULA
$$\bar{x} \pm t_{\alpha/2,\; n-1} \cdot \frac{s}{\sqrt{n}}$$
z vs t
As $n \to \infty$, $t \to z$
Wider than z-interval for small $n$
Extra uncertainty from estimating $\sigma$
CONDITIONS
$\sigma$ is unknown
Normal pop. or $n \ge 30$
Random sample

WORKED EXAMPLE

Battery Life: z-Interval

A manufacturer tests 36 batteries. The sample mean lifetime is 48.5 hours. From historical data, the population standard deviation is known: σ = 5.2 hours. Construct a 95% CI for the true mean battery life.

STEP 1 — DEFINE
Let $\mu \triangleq$ true mean battery life (hours)
Goal: 95% CI for $\mu$
STEP 2 — IDENTIFY
Z-Interval
$\sigma$ known, $n=36 \ge 30$ (CLT), random sample
STEP 3 — COMPUTE
$$\bar{x} \pm z_{0.025} \cdot \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}} = 48.5 \pm 1.96 \cdot \frac{5.2}{\sqrt{36}} = 48.5 \pm 1.699$$
$$\boxed{CI:\;(46.80,\; 50.20)\text{ hours}}$$
STEP 4 — INTERPRET
"We are 95% confident the true mean battery life is between 46.80 and 50.20 hours."

WORKED EXAMPLE

Light Bulb Lifespan: t-Interval

A sample of n = 25 bulbs has $\bar{x} = 980$ hours, $s = 50$ hours. Construct a 95% CI for the true mean lifespan. ($\sigma$ is unknown.)

STEP 1 — GIVEN
$n = 25$, $\bar{x} = 980$, $s = 50$
σ unknown → use t-interval
STEP 2 — CRITICAL VALUE
df = $n{-}1 = 24$
$t_{0.025,\; 24} = \mathbf{2.064}$
STEP 3 — COMPUTE
$$\text{ME} = t_{\alpha/2,\; n-1} \cdot \frac{s}{\sqrt{n}} = 2.064 \times \frac{50}{\sqrt{25}} = 2.064 \times 10 = 20.64$$
$$\boxed{CI:\;(959.36,\; 1000.64)\text{ hours}}$$
STEP 4 — INTERPRET
"We are 95% confident the true mean lifespan is between 959.36 and 1000.64 hours."

CONFIDENCE INTERVAL

CI for a Proportion

FORMULA
$$\hat{p} \pm z_{\alpha/2} \sqrt{\frac{\hat{p}(1 - \hat{p})}{n}}$$
CONDITIONS
$n\hat{p} \ge 5$ ✓
$n(1 - \hat{p}) \ge 5$ ✓
Random sample
⚠
Why these conditions? The normal approximation to the binomial requires enough successes and failures. If either $n\hat{p}$ or $n(1{-}\hat{p})$ is below 5, use an exact method instead.

Defect Rate: Proportion CI

THE FRAMEWORK

What Is Hypothesis Testing?

Assume the status quo ($H_0$) is true until data provides strong enough evidence against it.

HYPOTHESES
$H_0$: Null — no effect, status quo
$H_1$: Alternative — what we suspect
THREE TAIL TYPES
Left-tailed: $H_1: \mu < \mu_0$
Right-tailed: $H_1: \mu > \mu_0$
Two-tailed: $H_1: \mu \ne \mu_0$
LEFT-TAILED
$H_1: \mu < \mu_0$
TWO-TAILED
$H_1: \mu \ne \mu_0$
RIGHT-TAILED
$H_1: \mu > \mu_0$

CRITICAL CONCEPT

Two Approaches to HT

Both approaches always give the same decision. Choose the one your problem calls for.

REJECTION REGION (Critical Value)
1. Find critical value(s) from table
2. Compute test statistic
3. If test stat falls in rejection region → reject
P-VALUE
1. Compute test statistic
2. Calculate p-value from distribution
3. If p-value $< \alpha$ → reject
Both approaches give the same answer. The p-value approach is more common in practice because it tells you how strong the evidence is.

PROCEDURE

5-Step HT Procedure

1
State hypotheses
Write $H_0$ and $H_1$ in terms of the population parameter
2
Choose significance level $\alpha$
Common choices: 0.10, 0.05, 0.01. Must be set before looking at data
3
Calculate test statistic
$z = \frac{\bar{x} - \mu_0}{\sigma/\sqrt{n}}$ or $t = \frac{\bar{x} - \mu_0}{s/\sqrt{n}}$
4
Find p-value or critical value
Use z-table, t-table, or software. Compare to $\alpha$
5
State conclusion in context
"At the $\alpha$ level, there is [sufficient/insufficient] evidence to conclude..."

KEY CONCEPT

What Is a P-value?

The p-value is the probability of observing data this extreme or more extreme, assuming $H_0$ is true.
p < 0.01
Very strong evidence against $H_0$
0.01 ≤ p < 0.05
Moderate evidence against $H_0$
p ≥ 0.05
Weak or no evidence against $H_0$

SUMMARY

Test Statistic Formulas

z-TEST
($\sigma$ known)
$$z = \frac{\bar{x} - \mu_0}{\sigma / \sqrt{n}}$$
t-TEST
($\sigma$ unknown)
$$t = \frac{\bar{x} - \mu_0}{s / \sqrt{n}}$$
PROPORTION
(large sample)
$$z = \frac{\hat{p} - p_0}{\sqrt{\frac{p_0(1{-}p_0)}{n}}}$$
💡
Key insight: All test statistics follow the same pattern: $\dfrac{\text{observed} - \text{hypothesized}}{\text{standard error}}$. How many standard errors away is our data from $H_0$?

WORKED EXAMPLE

Coffee Machine: z-Test (Both Approaches)

A coffee machine dispenses $\mu_0 = 12$ oz. A sample of n = 25 cups has $\bar{x} = 11.7$ oz. Known $\sigma = 0.8$ oz. At α = 0.05, is the machine dispensing less than claimed? ($H_1: \mu < 12$)

STEP 1-2: HYPOTHESES & TEST STAT
$H_0: \mu = 12$ vs $H_1: \mu < 12$ (left-tailed)
$$z = \frac{11.7 - 12}{0.8/\sqrt{25}} = \frac{-0.3}{0.16} = -1.875$$
REJECTION REGION APPROACH
Critical value: $z_{0.05} = -1.645$ (left-tail)
$z = -1.875 < -1.645$ → in rejection region
Reject $H_0$
P-VALUE APPROACH
$p\text{-value} = P(Z < -1.875) = 0.0304$
$0.0304 < 0.05$ → Reject $H_0$
CONCLUSION
"At the 5% level, there is sufficient evidence that the coffee machine dispenses less than 12 oz."
Both methods agree: reject $H_0$.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Marketing Campaign: Proportion Test

A company claims 30% respond to ads. New campaign: 84 out of 250 responded. At α = 0.05, is the new rate different?

STEP 1 — HYPOTHESES
$$H_0: p = 0.30 \qquad H_1: p \ne 0.30$$
Two-tailed
STEP 2 — TEST STATISTIC
$\hat{p} = 84/250 = 0.336$
$$z = \frac{0.336 - 0.30}{\sqrt{0.30 \times 0.70 / 250}} = \boxed{1.24}$$
STEP 3 — DECISION (BOTH APPROACHES)
Critical Value
$z_{\text{crit}} = \pm 1.96$ → $|1.24| < 1.96$
Fail to Reject
P-value
$p = 2 \times P(Z > 1.24) = 0.215$
$0.215 > 0.05$ → Fail to Reject
"At the 5% significance level, there is insufficient evidence that the new campaign response rate differs from 30%."

TWO SIDES OF ONE COIN

Confidence Interval vs Hypothesis Test

If $\mu_0$ falls outside the $(1{-}\alpha)$ CI, you reject $H_0$ at level $\alpha$.
If $\mu_0$ falls inside the CI, you fail to reject.

TWO-SAMPLE TESTS

Two-Sample t-Test

TEST STATISTIC (Welch's)
$$t = \frac{\bar{x}_1 - \bar{x}_2}{\sqrt{\frac{s_1^2}{n_1} + \frac{s_2^2}{n_2}}}$$
WELCH'S df
$$\text{df} \approx \frac{\left(\frac{s_1^2}{n_1}+\frac{s_2^2}{n_2}\right)^2}{\frac{(s_1^2/n_1)^2}{n_1-1}+\frac{(s_2^2/n_2)^2}{n_2-1}}$$
Does not assume equal variances

WORKED EXAMPLE

Assembly Lines: Two-Sample t

Machine A: $n_1=20$, $\bar{x}_1=52.3$ min, $s_1=4.1$. Machine B: $n_2=22$, $\bar{x}_2=49.8$ min, $s_2=3.6$. At $\alpha=0.05$, is there a difference in assembly time?

HYPOTHESES & TEST STAT
$H_0: \mu_1 = \mu_2$ vs $H_1: \mu_1 \ne \mu_2$
$$t = \frac{52.3 - 49.8}{\sqrt{\frac{4.1^2}{20}+\frac{3.6^2}{22}}} = \frac{2.5}{\sqrt{0.841+0.589}} = \frac{2.5}{1.196} = 2.09$$
DECISION
Welch's df $\approx 38$
$t_{0.025,\,38} \approx 2.024$
$|2.09| > 2.024$ → Reject $H_0$
$p\text{-value} \approx 0.043 < 0.05$
"At the 5% level, there is sufficient evidence that Machine A and Machine B have different mean assembly times."

PAIRED DATA

Paired t-Test

WHEN TO USE
Same subjects measured twice (before/after)
Matched pairs (twins, left/right hand)
Reduces variability by focusing on differences
FORMULA
$d_i = x_{i,\text{after}} - x_{i,\text{before}}$
$$t = \frac{\bar{d}}{s_d / \sqrt{n}}$$
df = $n - 1$ (number of pairs)
EXAMPLE: TRAINING PROGRAM
10 employees tested before and after a training program. Mean difference $\bar{d} = 4.2$ points, $s_d = 3.1$. At $\alpha = 0.05$, did the training improve scores? ($H_1: \mu_d > 0$)
$$t = \frac{4.2}{3.1/\sqrt{10}} = \frac{4.2}{0.980} = 4.29 \quad \text{df}=9, \;\; t_{0.05,9}=1.833$$
$4.29 > 1.833$ → Reject $H_0$. Strong evidence the training improved scores.

What You Now Know

CI FORMULAS
Mean ($\sigma$ known): $\bar{x} \pm z_{\alpha/2} \cdot \sigma/\sqrt{n}$
Mean ($\sigma$ unknown): $\bar{x} \pm t_{\alpha/2} \cdot s/\sqrt{n}$
Proportion: $\hat{p} \pm z_{\alpha/2}\sqrt{\hat{p}(1{-}\hat{p})/n}$
Variance: $\chi^2$ interval, df=$n{-}1$
TEST STATISTICS
z-test: $z = (\bar{x}-\mu_0)/(\sigma/\sqrt{n})$
t-test: $t = (\bar{x}-\mu_0)/(s/\sqrt{n})$
Proportion: $z = (\hat{p}-p_0)/\sqrt{p_0(1{-}p_0)/n}$
Two-sample: Welch's t   Paired: $t = \bar{d}/(s_d/\sqrt{n})$
CI ↔ HT CONNECTION
If $\mu_0$ falls outside the $(1{-}\alpha)$ CI, you reject $H_0$ at level $\alpha$. Two sides of the same coin.
