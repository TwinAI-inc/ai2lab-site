Confidence Intervals & Hypothesis Testing on Real Data

Find a real world dataset, pose four statistical questions about it, and answer them with four formal tests: one for a mean, one for the difference of two means, one for a standard deviation, and one ANOVA across 3+ groups. Submit a tight, two page report.

Max 2 pages Real data, your choice 4 tests required GenAI bonus 100 points
Overview
What you will do
This project ties together the Confidence Intervals & Hypothesis Testing deck with a dataset you pick yourself. You will build CIs and run three formal tests on real data, then report the results like a working analyst.
Step 1
Pick a dataset from a reputable source (see the list below). It must have at least one continuous numeric variable, one categorical variable with 2 groups (n ≥ 30 each), and one categorical variable with 3+ groups for the ANOVA.
Step 2
Pose four questions that map to the four required tests. Each question should be something a decision maker would actually ask about your data.
Step 3
Run, interpret, and report. Each test gets a clean hypothesis, a confidence interval, a p-value, a decision, and a one-line interpretation a non-statistician would understand.
Ground rules
Dataset requirements
Pick responsibly — the quality of your tests is only as good as the quality of your data.
Must have
  • • At least one continuous numeric variable (your test variable)
  • • A 2-group categorical variable (for Test 2)
  • • A 3+ group categorical variable (for ANOVA in Test 4) — can be the same variable split into more levels
  • • n ≥ 30 per group (so CLT kicks in)
  • • A credible public source you can cite with a URL and access date
  • • Data you actually understand — units, definitions, collection method
Don't do
  • • Don't use a textbook sample dataset (iris, titanic, mpg, etc.)
  • • Don't re-use a dataset from a classmate or prior project
  • • Don't generate synthetic data — tests must be on real observations
  • • Don't pick a tiny sample just to make the math easier
  • • Don't test a hypothesis you already know the answer to

The core of the project
Four required tests
Each test must be framed as a real question about your dataset, run correctly, and interpreted in plain English. Report the CI and the p-value for each.
1
One sample test for a mean
Is the population mean different from some benchmark value $\mu_0$?
t-test or z-test
Hypotheses
$H_0: \mu = \mu_0$
$H_1: \mu \neq \mu_0$ (or one sided)
Test statistic
$t = \dfrac{\bar{x} - \mu_0}{s / \sqrt{n}}$   with $df = n-1$
95% CI for $\mu$
$\bar{x} \pm t_{\alpha/2,\;n-1} \cdot \dfrac{s}{\sqrt{n}}$
Example question
"Is the average daily high temperature in Boston this year different from the historical average of 58 °F?"
2
Two sample test for a difference of means
Do two groups have different population means?
Independent samples t-test
Hypotheses
$H_0: \mu_1 = \mu_2$
$H_1: \mu_1 \neq \mu_2$
Test statistic (Welch)
$t = \dfrac{\bar{x}_1 - \bar{x}_2}{\sqrt{\dfrac{s_1^2}{n_1} + \dfrac{s_2^2}{n_2}}}$
95% CI for $\mu_1 - \mu_2$
$(\bar{x}_1 - \bar{x}_2) \pm t_{\alpha/2} \cdot SE$
Example question
"Do homes in ZIP code A sell for more per square foot than in ZIP code B?"
3
Test for a standard deviation (or variance)
Is the population spread different from some benchmark $\sigma_0$?
Chi-squared test
Hypotheses
$H_0: \sigma^2 = \sigma_0^2$
$H_1: \sigma^2 \neq \sigma_0^2$ (or one sided)
Test statistic
$\chi^2 = \dfrac{(n-1)s^2}{\sigma_0^2}$   with $df = n-1$
95% CI for $\sigma^2$
$\left( \dfrac{(n-1)s^2}{\chi^2_{\alpha/2}},\; \dfrac{(n-1)s^2}{\chi^2_{1-\alpha/2}} \right)$
Example question
"Is the day to day variability in a stock's return larger than the sector benchmark SD of 1.5%?"
4
One way ANOVA across 3+ groups
Do the means across three or more groups differ from each other?
F-test
Hypotheses
$H_0: \mu_1 = \mu_2 = \ldots = \mu_k$
$H_1$: at least one $\mu_i$ differs
Test statistic
$F = \dfrac{MS_B}{MS_W}$   with $(k{-}1,\; N{-}k)$ df
If you reject $H_0$
Follow up with Tukey's HSD to locate which specific pairs differ. Report $\eta^2 = SS_B / SS_T$ as the effect size.
Example question
"Do average home prices differ across 3 Boston neighborhoods (Back Bay, South End, Brighton)?"
Assumptions checklist (for every test): independence of observations, approximate normality (or n ≥ 30 for CLT), and equal variances for Tests 2 & 4 (or use Welch's correction / Kruskal-Wallis). State each one in your report and say how you checked it (histogram, QQ plot, Levene).
Starter ideas
Sample project ideas
Use these as inspiration, but bring your own angle. The best projects pick a question you actually care about the answer to. For each idea, the 2-group split works for Test 2 — expand to 3 or more groups for the ANOVA in Test 4.
IDEA 1
Housing prices in two ZIP codes
Pull Zillow data for two neighborhoods. Test (1) whether average price per sqft in ZIP A differs from the regional benchmark, (2) whether ZIP A vs ZIP B differ, and (3) whether ZIP A is more price volatile than the regional SD.
Zillow Researchhousing
IDEA 2
NFL quarterback performance
Download QB stats for a recent season. Test (1) whether mean passer rating differs from a historical league value, (2) whether AFC vs NFC QBs differ, and (3) whether the SD of yards per attempt is different from the league benchmark.
Pro Football Referencesports
IDEA 3
NHANES health measurement
Pick a variable like systolic blood pressure. Test (1) whether the mean differs from the CDC target of 120 mmHg, (2) whether men vs women differ, and (3) whether SD in one subgroup exceeds a clinical benchmark.
CDC NHANEShealth
IDEA 4
City climate comparison
Pull daily high temperatures for two cities for the past year from NOAA. Test (1) whether Boston's mean high differs from the 30 year normal, (2) whether Boston vs Chicago differ, and (3) whether Boston's SD (weather variability) differs from a baseline.
NOAA CDOclimate
IDEA 5
Stock return behavior
Pull daily returns for two tickers for the last 2 years from Yahoo Finance or FRED. Test (1) whether the mean daily return differs from 0, (2) whether the two tickers have different means, and (3) whether one ticker's SD (volatility) differs from the sector benchmark.
Yahoo Finance / FREDfinance
IDEA 6
Airline on time performance
Use BTS Reporting Carrier On Time data. Test (1) whether mean delay at Logan differs from the national average, (2) whether two carriers differ, and (3) whether one carrier's delay SD (consistency) exceeds the national benchmark.
BTS / Data.govoperations
