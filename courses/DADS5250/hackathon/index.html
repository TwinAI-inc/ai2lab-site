Rebalancing Bluebikes Across Greater Boston

Planning bike rebalancing crews by city using real Bluebikes trip data.

Photo: Bluebikes station at Lansdowne, by 4300streetcar, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 4.0. Logo: Bluebikes, public domain.

Every weekday morning the bikes drain out of the neighborhoods and pile up downtown, and by the evening it runs the other way. Somebody has to drive a van around and move them back, and that somebody costs real money. In this project you decide how many rebalancing crews each city needs in each month of 2026, using two years of actual trip records. Then you build an AI application that puts that plan in the hands of the people who run the system each morning.

530,297trips in July 2026
606stations

Background

Bluebikes is the bike-share system for Boston and neighboring cities such as Cambridge, Somerville, and Brookline. Riders have taken over 31 million trips since it launched in 2011. In July 2026 alone, riders took 530,297 trips from 606 stations, about 17,000 a day.

Riders don't return bikes evenly. In July 2026, the station at Ames St at Main St in Kendall Square received 1,230 more bikes than left it, while Government Center lost 480 and MIT Vassar St lost 415. When a station runs empty, riders can't find a bike. When it fills up, riders can't return one. Either way, trips and revenue are lost.

Rebalancing crews drive vans that move bikes from full stations to empty ones. Demand changes a lot with the season, the weather, the academic calendar, and events like Red Sox games, the Boston Marathon, and September move-in.

Your goal is to build a month-by-month plan for 2026 that decides how many rebalancing crews each city needs, using real trip data from 2024 and 2025. You can then check your plan against what actually happened from January to September 2026.

Key rules and workflow

The trip and station data is REAL. Crew capacity, costs, and starting crew levels are planning assumptions ASSUMED given for this challenge, the same way a business case gives salaries. Use them as stated.
1

Stations & demand REAL

  • Each station has a fixed number of docks, listed in the station file.
  • Each station belongs to a city (the Municipality column in the station file). Plan crews by city.
  • The trip data shows every pickup and return, so you can measure how many bikes each station gains or loses by hour and by day.
2

Measuring rebalancing need

  • The data doesn't record empty or full stations, or lost trips. Your team must estimate them.
  • One simple approach: assume each station starts the day half full, track bikes in and out hour by hour, and count the bikes that would need to be moved to keep it between empty and full. State whatever method you use.
3

  • A crew works only inside its own city.
  • Crews in January 2026: Boston 6, Cambridge 4, Somerville 1, Brookline 1. All other cities share 2 crews.

Staffing Boston's 311 City Service Teams

Planning field crews for four City of Boston departments using real 311 service request data.

Photo: Boston City Hall, by Daderot, Wikimedia Commons, CC0. Seal: City of Boston, public domain.

When somebody in Boston reports a pothole, a dead streetlight, or a rat, a crew somewhere in the city has to go and deal with it. Some departments close almost everything on time. Parking enforcement closes half. In this project you work out how many field crews four departments need in each month of 2025, from the city's own 311 records, and then build an AI application that turns a resident's plain-English complaint into the right request type and the right queue.

306,756requests in 2024
840a day
50%on time at Transportation
4departments in scope

Background

Boston 311 is the city's service request system. Residents report problems like potholes, missed trash pickups, broken streetlights, illegal parking, and rodents through the BOS:311 app, a phone call, or the website. City workers also log issues they spot in the field. In 2024, the city received 306,756 requests, about 840 a day.

Each request is routed to a city department and a team queue, and most come with a target date to close it. Demand is uneven. Public Works peaks in August and September, Parks more than triples between January and June, and requests vary by neighborhood and type.

Performance also varies a lot. In 2024, Public Works closed 90% of its requests on time, Parks 93%, and Inspectional Services 72%. Transportation, which handles parking enforcement, closed only 50% on time.

Your goal is to build a month-by-month staffing plan for 2025 that decides how many field crews four key departments need, using real 311 data from 2023 and 2024.

Request flow

Cost of an overdue request ASSUMED

  • Each overdue request costs $20 in repeat calls, rework, and lost resident trust.
  • A request still open 30 days after its target date costs an extra $100.

Dynamic Agent Staffing Optimization

Staffing support agents for new Google advertisers, month by month, under uncertain demand. A problem contributed by Google.

Photo: Google headquarters, Dublin, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 2.0. Logo: Google, used to identify the contributor.

New advertisers sign up for Google Ads every day, and each one gets a human to help them through their first 60 days. Hire too few agents and advertisers sit waiting. Hire too many and you are paying people to be idle. In this project you decide how many support agents each country needs in each month of 2025, with demand that will not sit still, and then build an AI application that makes the decision explainable to whoever has to approve the headcount.

10advertisers per agent
60days of support
1 moramp-up for a new agent
40%cost to fire

Background

Millions of businesses advertise on Google, with thousands of new advertisers joining daily worldwide. To ensure excellent support, Google assigns dedicated agents to assist eligible advertisers for 60 days shortly after they sign up.

Agent staffing is managed locally in each country, with agents serving advertisers in their respective regions. Each agent can handle up to 10 advertisers at any time, but the unpredictable daily influx of new accounts makes staffing optimization a challenge. Overstaffing leads to idle capacity and reduced ROI, while understaffing delays advertiser support and reduces incremental revenue.

Your goal is to develop a dynamic month-by-month staffing plan that ensures efficient support while optimizing costs.

At a glance

Key rules and workflow

1

Advertiser eligibility

  • When an advertiser signs up, Google projects its potential annual advertising budget.
  • If the budget exceeds a country-specific threshold, the advertiser becomes eligible for agent support.
  • Otherwise, it is ineligible and left untreated.
2

Agent assignment & capacity

  • Eligible advertisers are placed in a waiting pool.
  • If agent capacity is available, an agent is assigned.
  • If no capacity is available, the advertiser waits up to 60 days in the pool.
  • If still unassigned after 60 days, the advertiser is removed from the pool.
3

Agent lifecycle

  • Each agent manages a maximum of 10 advertisers at any point.
  • You can assume that all agents start 2026 with no advertisers already assigned to them.
  • Advertisers receive support for 60 days before graduating, which frees agent capacity.
  • New agents require 1 month of ramp-up time before they become available.
  • Firing an agent requires 1 month's advance notice and will incur a cost of 40% of the agent's annual salary.

Questions already asked

Answers from the team that contributed this problem.

In the Key Rules & Workflow section under Advertiser Eligibility, it mentions that the platform projects each advertiser's potential annual advertising budget. Does this mean the potential annual advertising budget has already been determined, or do we need to estimate it ourselves?
This means that the platform has already forecasted the potential advertising budget for the customer, and therefore there is no need for the team to estimate it separately.
Is there a growth rate parameter available for advertisers?
Assuming you are referring to growth in the annual advertising budget. You can assume that they are fixed throughout the year.
If an agent is serving 9 advertisers at day t, can we allow them to serve another advertiser at day t+20, for instance? If so, do all the advertisers under their support graduate at day t+80 before the agent enters the ramp-up period?
You can allow the agent to serve another advertiser as long as the total number of advertisers served by the agent does not exceed 10 at any point in time. Advertisers will graduate after 60 days of being served by an agent, and therefore advertisers served by the same agent may not graduate at the same time because they may have been picked up by the agent at a different point in time.
The first two datasets in the document (Annual Agent Salary per country in USD and Advertiser Eligibility Threshold per country in USD) appear to be identical. Is this a mistake?
This is intended because both pieces of information are documented in the same table in the same spreadsheet.
Can we hire or fire agents at any point in the middle of the month, or do we have to assume that we can only change staffing levels at the beginning of each month?
You can hire or fire agents at any point in the month, but ramp up time and advance notice still apply as laid out in the case prompt: new agents require 1 month of ramp-up time before they become available, and firing an agent requires 1 month's advance notice and will incur a cost of 40% of the agent's annual salary.
Are we expected to create a poster to showcase our solutions, or can we use another presentation format?
You are free to choose the format as for how you showcase your analysis and recommendations, for example a poster, a PowerPoint presentation, or a document.
Can we connect or add external datasets to the ones provided?
Yes, you are free to connect or use any external data. But please provide the rationale and justification on why you believe this external data helps with addressing the case problem.
