Impact Lab: the card exchange

This is the in-class activity. You get a card with a question on it, you answer it out loud to one person, you swap cards, and you move on. The whole thing runs five minutes.
1Five minutes total Keep moving. The clock is the point, not an obstacle.
2Five or six people Swap your card every time, so the question changes under you.
3Thirty to sixty seconds Long enough for a claim and a reason. Not long enough to hedge.
Why the constraints

A short clock forces you to lead with the point

Given ten minutes most people describe their method and run out of time before the finding. Given forty seconds you say the finding first, because there is room for nothing else.

Three habits that do most of the work

Everything in the five makeovers reduces to these. They are worth more than any template.
Be specific

Say what you concluded, in a sentence someone could disagree with.

"Q4 2024 Revenue" becomes
"Q4 was our best quarter, and the holiday launch is why."
Add context

A number alone has no direction. Give it something to measure against.

"4.2 out of 5" becomes
"4.2 out of 5, up from 3.5, the highest in five years."
Make it memorable

Decide what they should still know tomorrow and put it first. Equal emphasis is also a decision.

four metrics in one size becomes
one of them large, the others beneath it.
