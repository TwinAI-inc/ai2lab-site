IE 4512 Engineering Economy
Course syllabus, prepared by Dr. Mohammad Dehghani.
i  ·  Course Overview
This course explores economic modeling and analysis techniques for selecting alternatives from potential solutions to an engineering problem. The course begins with measures of merit and basic methods of economic comparison—present worth, annual worth, rate of return, and benefit/cost techniques. We then examine more recent techniques of economic analysis and the tools of decision making, including risk and uncertainty, the effects of taxes on investment, decision tree analysis, statistical decision techniques, and multiple attributes of decision making through group case studies.
ii  ·  Course Objectives
a.Identify the key financial vehicles that affect how companies do business and describe their impact on economic decisions.
b.Analyze projects using standard economic decision making techniques such as breakeven and sensitivity analysis, risk and uncertainty, and decision trees.
c.Analyze and evaluate complex real world economic scenarios by applying multiple attributes of decision making principles.
d.Evaluate projects based on dynamic factors such as depreciation and taxes to choose the optimal alternatives.
e.Compare competing projects by understanding time value of money principles applied to cash flows.
f.Apply multi criteria decision making techniques to include economical and non economical factors in the alternatives selection process.
g.Determine economic equivalence and manipulate cash flows in order to make economic projections.

vi  ·  Components
Excel Assignments — 15%
Worked through in class alongside the lecture. Students follow along with the instructor, complete the spreadsheet, and submit by the end of each module on Canvas. Label submissions as IE4512 – Excel HW # <Student Name>. Worst Excel grade dropped.
Online Assignments — 15%
Take home homework problems posted on Canvas after each module. Additional problem solving exercises tied to the topics covered in lecture, designed to reinforce concepts beyond the in class Excel work. Worst online assignment grade dropped.
Quizzes — 20%
In class, paper based quizzes given throughout the term. Each quiz covers the current session as well as one or two prior sessions. Closed book unless announced otherwise. Worst quiz grade dropped. No makeup quizzes.
Participation & Presentation — 10%
Attendance is mandatory. Each team is assigned a topic relevant to course content and delivers a short in class presentation tying it to engineering economy concepts. Evaluated on engagement, preparation, and presentation quality.
Mini Exam 1 & Mini Exam 2 — 25% combined (12.5% each)
Two take home exams announced 1 to 2 weeks in advance. Equally weighted at 12.5% each. Mini Exam 1 covers the first half of the course; Mini Exam 2 covers the second half. Submitted via Canvas with no extensions.
Final Project — 15%
Team capstone applying the full course toolkit (cash flow, present and annual worth, rate of return, depreciation, after tax analysis, decision under uncertainty) to a real world investment scenario. Deliverables: written report, Excel decision model, and final team presentation. Guidelines and rubric on Canvas.
