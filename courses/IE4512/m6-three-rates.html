Method 1 of 3

Internal · IRR

The discount rate that drives NPV exactly to zero for the project's cash flow. It is called "internal" because no outside rate is needed — the value is solved from the project alone.

Hidden assumption: every dollar the project pays out is reinvested at the IRR itself. If the firm cannot really earn that rate on incoming cash, IRR overstates the true return.
\[ \mathrm{NPV}(r) = \sum_{t=0}^{n} \frac{CF_t}{(1+r)^t} = 0 \]
solve r numerically (bisection or Newton-Raphson)
MARR · hurdle rate10.0%
used only for the accept/reject verdict on IRR

Method 2 of 3

External · ERR

Inflows are compounded forward to year n at the MARR. Outflows are discounted back to year 0 at the MARR. Then a single rate is solved that links the two collapsed values across n years.

How it fixes IRR: reinvestment happens at the firm's MARR, not at the project's own IRR. Always one solution, even for non-conventional cash flows.
\[ \mathrm{ERR} = \left(\frac{FW_{+}(i)}{PW_{-}(i)}\right)^{1/n} - 1 \]
both directions use i = MARR
MARR · used in calc & verdict10.0%
ERR moves with MARR — change it and watch the rate slide

Method 3 of 3

Modified · MIRR

Same shape as ERR, but with two different rates. Outflows discount at the firm's finance rate (cost of borrowing). Inflows compound at the reinvestment rate (what the firm can actually earn on incoming cash).

When to use: the firm borrows at one rate and reinvests at another. Excel computes it as MIRR(values, finance, reinvest).
\[ \mathrm{MIRR} = \left(\frac{FW_{+}(i_r)}{PW_{-}(i_f)}\right)^{1/n} - 1 \]
