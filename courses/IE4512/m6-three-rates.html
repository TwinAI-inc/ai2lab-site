Method 1

Internal · IRR

The single rate that makes the project's net present value exactly zero. No external assumptions.
\[ \mathrm{NPV}(\mathrm{IRR}) = \sum_{t=0}^{n} \frac{CF_t}{(1+\mathrm{IRR})^t} = 0 \]
solve for the rate, numerically (Newton-Raphson or bisection)

Method 2

External · ERR

Move every inflow forward to year n at the MARR, every outflow back to year 0 at the MARR, then solve for the rate that links them.
\[ \mathrm{ERR} = \left(\frac{FW_{+}(i)}{PW_{-}(i)}\right)^{1/n} - 1 \]
single rate i = MARR used for both directions

Method 3

Modified · MIRR

Same shape as ERR but uses two distinct rates: borrowing cost for the outflows, realistic reinvestment for the inflows.
\[ \mathrm{MIRR} = \left(\frac{FW_{+}(i_r)}{PW_{-}(i_f)}\right)^{1/n} - 1 \]
if = finance rate · ir = reinvestment rate

When do you reach for which?

Use IRR when
cash flows are conventional (one sign change), the project is small relative to the firm, and the implicit reinvestment-at-IRR assumption is plausible.
Use ERR when
cash flows are non-conventional, multiple IRRs are possible, and you want to be explicit that intermediate inflows are reinvested at the firm's MARR, not at the project's own IRR.
Use MIRR when
the firm's cost of borrowing differs from the rate it can reinvest at, and you want both realities to show up in one number. The Excel MIRR(values, finance_rate, reinvest_rate) function does this.
