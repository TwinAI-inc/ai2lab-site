Method 1

Internal · IRR

Rate that drives NPV to zero. Assumes reinvestment at the IRR itself.
\[ \mathrm{NPV}(r) = \sum_{t=0}^{n} \frac{CF_t}{(1+r)^t} = 0 \]
solve r numerically

Method 2

External · ERR

Inflows compound at MARR, outflows discount at MARR. One rate, no ambiguity.
\[ \mathrm{ERR} = \left(\frac{FW_{+}(i)}{PW_{-}(i)}\right)^{1/n} - 1 \]
both directions use i = MARR

Method 3

Modified · MIRR

Two rates — finance for outflows, reinvest for inflows. The realistic version.
\[ \mathrm{MIRR} = \left(\frac{FW_{+}(i_r)}{PW_{-}(i_f)}\right)^{1/n} - 1 \]
if finance · ir reinvest

The numbers on the table

Disney rebuilt the old Splash Mountain into a Tiana-themed log flume opening on the Splash bones. Imagine a stylized version where the build runs $120 M, the attraction earns $22 M per year in net ticket and merch attribution for ten years, and the project closes with $8 M of salvage value at the end of year 10.

Set the MARR slider on the previous page to 10% and watch the three rates split:

IRR says one number based on the project itself. ERR says a smaller, more honest number because reinvestment happens at the firm's MARR, not at the project's own internal rate. MIRR can land different again if the finance and reinvest rates diverge.
