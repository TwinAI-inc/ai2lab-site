Plan 1

Interest Only

Each year you pay just the accrued interest (constant = i · P). The full principal sits untouched until the very last year, when you pay it as one final lump.

Payment rule At = i · P  (yr 1 to n−1)  ·  An = i · P + P

Plan 2

Equal Principal

You retire a fixed chunk of principal each year (P/n). Interest is charged on the remaining balance, so payments are largest at the start and shrink each year.

Payment rule At = P/n + i · Bt−1  (B = remaining balance)

Plan 3

Equal Payment

The classic amortized loan. Your annual payment is constant. Inside each payment, the interest portion shrinks and the principal-paydown portion grows as the balance shrinks.

Payment rule A = P · i / [1 − (1 + i)−n]  ·  (A/P, i, n) factor

Plan 4

Single Payment

No periodic payments. Interest compounds yearly on the unpaid balance. At year n you owe the original P grown by (F/P, i, n).

Payment rule At = 0 (yr 1 to n−1)  ·  An = P · (1 + i)n

Insight

All four plans pay back the same loan, but their cash flow timing differs. The borrower's PW depends on the lender's rate (which sets the payments) and the borrower's TVOM (which discounts them).

The four curves cross at zero when Lender's Rate = Borrower's TVOM. There, every plan is equivalent — the borrower neither gains nor loses in PW.

To the left (Lender's Rate < TVOM): borrower benefits, and deferring payments amplifies the win. Preference: 4 ≻ 1 ≻ 3 ≻ 2.

To the right (Lender's Rate > TVOM): borrower loses, and deferring deepens the loss. Preference flips: 2 ≻ 3 ≻ 1 ≻ 4.
