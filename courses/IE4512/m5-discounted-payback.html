The question. Same engineering ambition, same era of finance, both opened on schedule. Yet one project's discounted cash flow climbed to zero and the other's stalled forever. What is the metric that tells these two stories apart — and how do you compute it before you spend the money?

What is the Discounted Payback Period?

Same discounting as Present Worth — but the question is the calendar, not the verdict. When am I made whole, in present-worth terms?
2 / 3 · define
Definition
DPP is the smallest n such that
∑t=0..n At · (P/F, i, t)  ≥  0
i.e., the year the cumulative discounted cash flow first turns non-negative at the MARR.
Plain Payback

Sums raw cash flows until they reach the initial cost. Ignores the time value of money.

Discounted Payback

Sums present-worth equivalents. Late dollars count less. Hence DPP > PP for any positive MARR.

Decision rule

Accept if DPP ≤ the analyst's max allowable payback window. Reject if the curve never reaches zero within the planning horizon.

DPP vs. PW

Same discount factors, different question. A project can have PW > 0 but a DPP longer than the planning horizon.

What DPP is good for

Liquidity and risk. "How long is my capital exposed?" — not a profitability ranker. That is PW's job.

Ranking with DPP — use the ranking method, not incremental

Compute each project's DPP independently at the MARR and pick the shortest one that meets the planning horizon. Do not form Δ(B−A) cash flows the way you would for IRR.
3 / 3 · rank
Procedure

The ranking method for DPP

  1. For each alternative, compute the cumulative discounted cash flow at the MARR.
  2. Read off DPP as the year the cumulative first turns non-negative.
  3. Discard any alternative whose DPP exceeds the planning horizon.
  4. Among survivors, pick the one with the shortest DPP — that is your DPP-best choice.
  5. Cross-check with PW. If DPP and PW disagree on a mutually exclusive choice, PW wins. DPP is a liquidity overlay, not the profitability metric.

Worked example · three mutually exclusive projects

DPP-best = A (3.95 yr)  |  PW-best = B (+$3 066). They disagree. All three survive the 6-yr screen; A returns capital fastest, but B creates 5× the present-worth wealth. For a mutually exclusive choice, PW is the tie-breaker. A's short DPP buys liquidity, not value. Note we never formed Δ(B−A) incremental cash flows.
Why not incremental?

Incremental analysis works for IRR because the return on (B − A) genuinely answers "is the extra investment in B worth it?". DPP has no such interpretation — the year a difference-in-cash-flows series first turns non-negative is not any alternative's payback. Mixing cash flows across projects produces a number with no decision meaning.

When DPP and PW disagree

DPP rewards front-loaded projects; PW rewards total wealth. In the table, A has the shortest DPP (3.95 yr) but B has 5× the PW. In a mutually exclusive choice, PW is the economically correct tie-breaker. Treat DPP as a risk filter, not a ranker.
