What is the Discounted Payback Period?

Same discounting as Present Worth — but the question is the calendar, not the verdict. Slide the MARR and watch the payback shift.
2 / 3 · define & play
Definition
DPP is the smallest n such that
$$\mathrm{DPP} \;=\; \min\!\left\{\, n \;:\; \sum_{t=0}^{n} \frac{A_t}{(1+i)^t} \;\ge\; 0 \,\right\}$$
i.e., the year the cumulative discounted cash flow first turns non-negative at the MARR.
1
Plain Payback sums raw cash flows. It ignores the time value of money.
2
Discounted Payback sums present-worth equivalents. Late dollars count less.
3
For any positive MARR, DPP > PP. That gap is the cost of money — slide the MARR and see it grow.

Three projects on the same map · DPP vs. PW

Cost $10 000 each · MARR = 10% · 6-yr horizon
They disagree. DPP picks A for the fastest capital return; PW picks B for five times the wealth. In a mutually exclusive choice, PW wins. A's short DPP buys liquidity, not value — treat DPP as a risk filter, not a ranker.
!
Why not incremental, like for IRR?

Incremental analysis works for IRR because the return on (B − A) genuinely answers "is the extra investment in B worth it?". DPP has no such interpretation — the year a difference-in-cash-flows series first turns non-negative is not any alternative's payback. Mixing cash flows across projects produces a number with no decision meaning. Rank each one on its own DPP, then cross-check with PW.
