\section{Course Overview}

This course explores economic modeling and analysis techniques for selecting alternatives from potential solutions to an engineering problem. The course begins with exploring measures of merit and basic methods of economic comparison, such as present worth, annual worth, rate of return, and benefit/cost techniques. Further moving on to examine recent techniques of economic analysis, especially the tools of decision-making. Along with that, the course considers causes of risk and uncertainty , effects of taxes on investment, decision tree analysis, statistical decision techniques, and multiple attributes of decision making through group case studies.

%------------------------------------------------------------
%	 Course Objectives
%------------------------------------------------------------
\vspace{0.1 cm} 
\section{Course Objectives}
\vspace{0.05 cm} 
%use \begin{outline} or \begin{outline}[enumerate] to create a list with subitems. 
\begin{itemize}[label= \color{myCOLOR}{$\RHD$}]
        \item Identify the key financial vehicles that effect how companies do business and describe their impact on economic decisions.
        \vspace{0.1 cm}
        \item Analyze projects and ventures using standard economic decision-making techniques such as breakeven and sensitivity analysis, risk analysis and uncertainty, decision trees, and other statistical decision techniques problem solving.
        \vspace{0.1 cm}
        \item Analyze and evaluate a variety of complex real -world economic scenarios by applying  multiple attributes of decision-making principles.
        \vspace{0.1 cm}
        \item Evaluate projects based on dynamic factors such as depreciation and taxes to choose the optimal alternatives.
        \item Be able to compare competing projects by understanding time value of money principles to cash flows.
        \item Apply multi-criteria decision-making techniques to include economical and non-economical factors into the alternatives selection process.
        \item Determine the economic equivalence and manipulate cash flows in order to make economic projections.
\end{itemize}

\section{Academic Integrity}
\vspace{0.05cm}

A commitment to the principles of academic integrity is essential to the mission of Northeastern University. The promotion of independent and original scholarship ensures that students derive the most from their educational experience and their pursuit of knowledge. Academic dishonesty violates the most fundamental values of an intellectual community and undermines the achievements of the entire University.

As members of the academic community, students must become familiar with their rights and responsibilities. In each course, they are responsible for knowing the requirements and restrictions regarding research and writing, examinations of whatever kind, collaborative work, the use of study aids, the appropriateness of assistance, and other issues. Students are responsible for learning the conventions of documentation and acknowledgment of sources in their fields. Northeastern University expects students to complete all examinations, tests, papers, creative projects, and assignments of any kind according to the highest ethical standards, as set forth either explicitly or implicitly in this Code or by the direction of instructors. 

Go to Academic Integrity \href{http://www.northeastern.edu/osccr/academic-integrity-policy/}{\color{myCOLOR}{\underline{website}}}  to access the full academic integrity policy.
