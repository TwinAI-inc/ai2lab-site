What does the IRS think this building is worth?

In 1931, in the depths of the Great Depression, a syndicate spent a year and a day building the tallest thing on Earth. They put it on the books, started depreciating it, and never stopped. Write down your guess for each blank below, then tap to reveal.
1
Total cost to build it in 1931: $ ___ M
tap
Final cost was reported at $40,948,900 (Empire State Building Inc., 1931 audited figures). Roughly $815 M in 2024 dollars adjusted for CPI. About 18% under budget despite a one-year build.
2
Tax book value on the firm's depreciation schedule today: ≈ $ ___
tap
Essentially zero. After 94 years of straight-line, ADR, ACRS and MACRS layered on top of one another, the original 1931 basis was fully recovered by the early 1980s. Subsequent rehab basis (new HVAC, the 2018 lobby reconstruction, antennas) carries its own MACRS clock but the building shell itself is gone from the books.
3
Market value today (Empire State Realty Trust 2024 disclosure): $ ___ B
tap
Roughly $2.3 B as a standalone asset (the larger Empire State Realty Trust market cap is ~$3 B and the building is the dominant asset). Annual rent roll ≈ $250 M. Observation deck alone clears ~$130 M per year.
The lesson · page 1

An asset can be worth zero on the books and a fortune in the world. Depreciation is not the same thing as the asset wearing out — it is a tax accounting choice. Empire State outlived its depreciable life by decades and is still earning rent every day. Book value ≠ market value.

Story · part 2 of 3 click each row to reveal

The casino that died on schedule

The Mirage was the most expensive hotel ever built in 1989. The IRS gives nonresidential real property a 39-year class life. Walking past it in 2024, would you have guessed it was already at the end of its tax life?
1
Total cost to build (1989): $ ___ M
tap
$630 M, the most expensive hotel in the world at the time. Bankrolled by junk-bond financing from Michael Milken at Drexel Burnham. Steve Wynn's wager: that Las Vegas could be re-imagined as a destination resort, not just a gambling stop.
2
IRS class life for nonresidential real property under MACRS: ___ years
tap
39 years exactly (post-1993 buildings; the Mirage was actually placed in service in late 1989 under the then-current 31.5-year class). Either way, the wrecking ball was scheduled.
3
Year demolished to make way for Hard Rock Las Vegas: ___
tap
Demolition began in 2024 — about 35 years after opening. The structure was fully depreciated. The land underneath, the most valuable acreage on the Strip, kept all of the value.
The lesson · page 2

Sometimes depreciation lands right on top of the asset's economic life. The Mirage hit zero on the books and got knocked down within a year of doing so. But the land never depreciates. When the structure went to zero, the basis didn't disappear — it migrated into the land, which Hard Rock paid a fortune for. Depreciate the building. Never the dirt.

"Queen of the Skies" · 1,574 built · 53-year production run
Story · part 3 of 3 click each row to reveal

Salvage value is a decision, not a guess

Boeing rolled the last 747 off the line in December 2022 after 53 years of production. But the planes did not retire when Boeing did. They went into a second life that depreciation can't quite capture.
1
Years airlines depreciate a 747 over (book life): ___ years
tap
Typically 20-25 years for major airlines (Delta, BA, Lufthansa) on a SLN schedule with a salvage assumption around 10-15% of cost. IRS MACRS class for commercial aircraft is 7 years — much shorter than book life, which is the source of permanent book/tax differences airlines disclose every quarter.
2
What happens after the passenger book life? ___
tap
Cargo conversion. The interior is gutted, a side cargo door is cut into the upper fuselage, and the plane flies another 10-15 years as a freighter for FedEx, UPS, Atlas Air, Cathay Pacific Cargo. Conversion cost: ~$30 M. Resulting useful life extension: huge.
3
Final destination once even the cargo life ends: _____ desert
tap
Mojave Boneyard (officially: Southern California Logistics Airport, Victorville) or Pinal Air Park in Arizona. The dry climate preserves the airframe. Engines, avionics, landing gear are stripped and resold for millions. The hulk goes to scrap aluminum for a few thousand dollars. Salvage value is whatever the airline negotiates at the end — it is not a number you wrote down in year 0.
The lesson · page 3

Salvage value is an active management decision, not a fixed input. The 747 has at least three depreciable lives stacked end to end — passenger, cargo, parts — and the airline chooses how much of each life to claim. The textbook says S is a constant. The world says S is a choice you make every year.
