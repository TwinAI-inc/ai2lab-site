GenDI · MCDM 2026
The Challenge

Traditional MCDM is powerful —
but bottlenecked.

Established methods (AHP, ANP, TOPSIS, DEMATEL) rely on human expert panels at every step.

Criteria identification Pairwise evaluation Alternative assessment
Pain 01
Cost
Expert time is expensive and difficult to coordinate at scale.
Pain 02
Consistency
Human judgment drifts; consistency ratios degrade with panel size.
Pain 03
Scalability
Large criteria sets become combinatorially unmanageable for humans.
Process

Six steps to a rigorous decision.

From defining the problem to ranked results — every step transparent and editable.

Step 01
Define
Describe your decision problem and list alternatives.
Step 02
Experts
AI generates a domain-specific virtual expert panel.
Step 03
Criteria
Identify and weight evaluation criteria via research.
Step 04
Compare
Pairwise comparisons performed by each expert.
Step 05
Analyze
Run AHP, ANP, TOPSIS, or DEMATEL on the inputs.
Step 06
Results
Rankings, sensitivity analysis, exportable reports.
Architecture

Inside GenDI.

Three layers: agentic experts feed evidence into a method-agnostic MCDM engine; the user supplies context and consumes results.

Standing on shoulders

Prof. Thomas L. Saaty.

Pioneer of the Analytic Hierarchy Process (1977) and the Analytic Network Process. His Saaty scale, consistency ratio, and pairwise comparison framework remain the backbone of modern MCDM — and the backbone of GenDI.
